1. What it does

Bag Tracker turns a list of what should be in a medical bag into printable A4 paperwork. It is for equipment and consumables — airways, dressings, bandages, shears — not medicines.

You describe the bag once, pouch by pouch, with a minimum quantity for each item. The tool then produces one PDF that can contain:

  • Bag labels — four cut-out labels for the outside of the bag, with the bag number and an “if found” notice.
  • A kit list — every pouch and what belongs in it, signed off by whoever packed and checked the bag.
  • A kit checklist — the same list with boxes to count and tick each item at a kit check, including seal numbers for sealed pouches.
  • A sign in / sign out log — who took the bag, when, where to, and when it came back.

Everything stays on your device. There is no account, no server and no upload. The page and the PDF library run entirely in your browser, so it works offline.

2. Getting started

Open index.html in any up-to-date browser — double-click it, or open the address where your organisation hosts it.

The work is split into three tabs, done in order:

  1. Setup — your company and the bag.
  2. Pouches — what is in the bag, and how many of each.
  3. Generate — choose the sheets and download the PDF.

Everything you type is saved in the browser as you go, so you can close the page and come back. Browser storage can be cleared, though — see section 9 for keeping a proper copy.

3. Setup

Start from a saved file

If you have exported a bag before, Import (.json) at the top of Setup loads it, including its pouches. This is the quickest way to set up a bag with the same layout as another.

Company details

  • Company name (required) — printed at the top of every sheet.
  • Contact number and address — used in the “if found” notice on the bag labels, and the number appears on the kit list.
  • Company logo — a PNG or JPEG, printed in the dark band at the top of each sheet and on each label. Large images are shrunk automatically.

4. Pouches and items

A bag is made of pouches (or modules), and each pouch holds items. Items kept loose in the bag can go in a pouch called something like “Main compartment”.

Adding a pouch

Type the pouch name and press Add pouch (or Enter). The new pouch appears below, ready for its items.

Adding items

  1. In the row at the bottom of the pouch, type the item, e.g. “OPA size 2”.
  2. Set the minimum quantity — the fewest that must be in the pouch for it to be ready for use.
  3. Press Add or Enter. The cursor goes back to the name box for the next item.

Put sizes in the item name (“NPA 6.0”, “Gloves (pairs, M)”) so each size is counted separately at a check.

Changing things

  • Rename a pouch or item, or change a minimum, by typing straight into its box. Changes save as you type.
  • ↑ / ↓ move an item within its pouch, or a pouch within the bag. The paperwork follows the same order, so put things in the order a crew would find them.
  • × removes an item. Remove removes a pouch — you are asked first if it still has items.

Sealed pouches

Some pouches are closed with a numbered tag. Tick Sealed with a tag on that pouch and enter the seal number. If the number changes every time the pouch is resealed, leave it blank and it is written in by hand.

On the paperwork, a sealed pouch is marked SEALED — SEAL No. on its heading, and the checklist adds a row for the seal number found, whether it was intact, and the new seal number.

5. Generating the paperwork

  1. Tick the sheets you want. Your choice is remembered with the bag.
  2. Set Checklist copies to print several blank checklists at once — useful for a month of daily checks.
  3. Press Download PDF, or Preview in new tab to look first.

If something needed is missing, the tool says what instead of making the PDF:

It needs a company name, a bag name, at least one item, a name on every pouch and item, and at least one sheet ticked.

6. What the PDF contains

The sheets come in this order. Every page has the bag number, company, kit list version and page number at the foot, so a page that goes astray can be put back.

Bag labels

Four identical labels on one page, with dashed lines to cut along. Each shows the company and logo, the bag number and name, the seal number, an If found box with your phone number and address, and “Medical equipment. Do not open unless authorised.”

Laminate one into the bag’s label window and keep the spares.

Kit list

The definitive list of what belongs in the bag: a strip of bag details, then each pouch as a dark heading followed by its numbered items and minimum quantities.

The Bag packed box at the bottom is signed by whoever packed the bag and whoever checked it.

Kit checklist

Used at each kit check. Write the date and seal numbers at the top, then for every item write how many were found, tick OK when it meets the minimum, and note anything replaced, short or out of date.

Sealed pouches have an extra row for the seal found, whether it was intact, and the new seal fitted.

Sign the Check completed box at the end. A second signature is there if your procedure needs one.

Sign in / sign out log

A landscape sheet that lives with the bag. Before the bag leaves, fill in the Signed out half: date, time, name, signature, the event or vehicle, and the seal number. When it comes back, fill in the Signed in half. If the seal is broken or the number has changed, check the bag against the kit checklist.

7. Several identical bags

If you run several bags with the same layout, you do not need to set each one up. In Bag number(s) on Setup, list them separated by commas:

RB-01, RB-02, RB-03

The PDF then holds a full set of the ticked sheets for RB-01, then RB-02, then RB-03, each with its own bag number on every page and label.

Seal numbers are left blank when there is more than one bag, because each bag has its own seals. Write them in by hand.

8. Keeping the kit list up to date

When a kit list changes, old printed checklists can linger in bags and vehicles. To make an out-of-date copy easy to spot, every page carries a line such as:

Kit list v3, updated 04/11/2026

  • The date moves to today by itself whenever you add, remove, rename or reorder anything in the pouches.
  • The version is yours to set. Raise it when you issue a new layout, and tell crews which version is current.

9. Saving, reusing and backing up

The bag is saved in this browser as you type, but browser storage is not a backup: clearing browsing data, a private window or a different computer will lose it.

  • Export (.json) on the Generate tab saves the whole bag — details, logo, pouches and sheet choices — to a file. Keep it somewhere durable, such as a shared drive.
  • Import (.json) loads a saved bag, replacing what is on screen. Export first if you want to keep the current one.
  • To set up a new bag based on an existing one, import it and change the name and number.

10. Phones and tablets

The tool works on a phone or tablet. The layout narrows to fit, and the dark theme follows your device’s setting.

Setting up a large bag is easier on a computer, but a phone is fine for a quick change and for downloading the PDF to print.

11. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
My bag has gone.Browser storage was cleared, or you are in a different browser or a private window. Import your last exported .json.
“Not a bag exported from this tool”The file is not a Bag Tracker export — for example a drug bag file from the drug bag tracker.
The PDF did not download.Read the red message on the Generate tab. Some browsers also ask before saving a download.
The logo will not load.Use a PNG or JPEG. A very large or complex image may be too big to store — try a smaller copy.
A long item name is cut off on screen.Only the box is too narrow. The full name is kept and printed in full on the paperwork.
The seal number is missing from the paperwork.Check Sealed with a tag is ticked on that pouch, and that there is only one bag number.

12. Disclaimer and licence

Bag Tracker is a document generator. It is not a clinical or equipment standard and does not tell you what a bag should contain. Your organisation decides that. Check every kit list before it is used.

Everything is stored in your browser and nothing is uploaded. No responsibility is accepted for work lost to browser storage being cleared — export your bags.
