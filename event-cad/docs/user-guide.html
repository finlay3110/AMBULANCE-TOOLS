1. What it does

The Event CAD is a computer-aided dispatch log for event medical control. On one laptop it keeps every call (where, what, who was sent and when they got there), where each unit is, the treatment centre counts and the control log. At the end it makes the event report, every call log as a PDF, and a spreadsheet of the calls.

  1. Setup — the company, the event, the staffing, the locations and hospitals. Done before the event, and saved to reuse.
  2. CAD — the live screen: calls, units and alerts.
  3. Treatment centre — the hourly count of patients in each centre.
  4. Control log — everything that is not a call, METHANE messages and casualty counts.
  5. Report — the report, the call logs, the spreadsheet, backups and finalising.

Everything stays on the laptop. There is no account and nothing is uploaded. That also means the laptop holds the only copy: see 8. Keeping the CAD safe before the event starts.

2. Before the event: setup

Set up your Company Profile first (Step 1 on the home page): the CAD picks it up by itself. Then, on the Setup tab:

Staffing

Each unit has a callsign, a type and the staff on it. Only an Ambulance can take a patient to hospital. Starts at is the standby point it is at when the event begins.

If you planned the event in Deployment Maker, load the units from there: the deployment open in Deployment Maker, a saved plan, or a Deployment Maker file. Their locations become standby points. Deployment Maker only knows which units are ambulances, so set the type of the others.

Locations and hospitals

Add the standby points, points of interest and treatment centres (as many as the event has), and the hospitals ambulances may go to. They become the choices on every call. Give a location its site map grid reference and what3words address, and both fill in by themselves when a call is at that location.

Alerts, targets, phrases and the site map

  • Alerts: how long a call can wait without a unit, and how long a unit can be on scene without an update before the CAD asks for a welfare check.
  • Response time targets: minutes from the call to the first unit on scene, per category. Leave blank for none.
  • what3words key (optional): a free key from what3words lets the CAD suggest real addresses as you type one. It stays on this laptop.
  • Quick log phrases: updates you log often, one per line; each becomes a one-tap button on every call.
  • Site map: a picture of the event’s site map, shown on the CAD tab. Set how many grid squares it has across (A, B, C…) and down (1, 2, 3…), and you can click the map to fill in a call’s grid reference, and the report shows where the calls were on the map.

Print the paper fallback pack

Download paper fallback pack prints a unit status sheet with your callsigns, a call list, a blank call sheet and METHANE form to copy, the site map, and your locations, hospitals and the MPDS protocols. If the laptop fails, control carries on with a pen.

Export setup saves all of this as a file, to reuse for the next event at the same venue or to set up another laptop.

3. Running control: the CAD screen

  • Controller on duty: type your name at the start of your shift. It goes on every call and log entry, and the change is logged. At a handover, the CAD asks for a handover note for the log.
  • The buttons stay at the top as you scroll. On an iPad held upright, or a narrow window, Board and Call switch between the board and the call sheet. Side by side, the call sheet stays in view while the board scrolls.
  • The summary strip under the buttons: calls, open calls, patients, conveyed to hospital, average response and units available, kept up to date.
  • SITREP writes a short situation report for the last hour or the whole event, to copy into a message, read over the radio, or add to the control log.
  • Alerts (amber): a call with no unit, a call past its target with no unit on scene, a welfare check, a break that has run over, a hospital on divert, a missing person, the hourly treatment centre count, and a reminder to export when there is no live backup file. A category 1 call with no unit and a missed welfare check also beep.
  • Open calls are listed category 1 first, then oldest. Closed calls are underneath. Search finds a call by number, callsign, place, PRF, NHS CAD number or any text.
  • Site map: open it under the open calls; click it for full screen, and click again (or press Escape) to close.

4. A call from start to finish

  1. + New call starts a call, numbered and timed. For someone who walks into a treatment centre, use + Walk-up instead; with more than one treatment centre, it asks which.
  2. Who and where: how it came in, who reported it, the point of interest, a description, the what3words address (with a link to the map) and the site map grid reference.
  3. What: the MPDS protocol, the category, the details, and the police, safeguarding and lost or vulnerable person ticks. With police involved, add their reference. A second report of the same incident is marked Duplicate of the first: it is kept but left out of the figures. Raising or lowering the category is written in the call’s log by itself.
  4. Patients: age, sex and PRF number for each, how they presented (minor injury, medical, intoxication and so on) and whether alcohol or drugs were involved. These give the report’s patient figures.
  5. Units: choose a unit and Assign, then tap each step as the crew report it.
  6. Close the call once its units are clear and each patient has an outcome.

Units on a call

Each unit moves through Assigned, En route, On scene, Moving patient, At destination, Clear, or Stood down at any point. Tap a step when the crew report it; the time is recorded. A time logged late can be corrected: click it.

Moving patient asks where to. An ambulance can go to Hospital: choose the hospital (a hospital on divert shows a warning), and tick Blue light / pre-alert for the ATMIST pre-alert, its ETA, who it was passed to, and whether it went direct or via NHS ambulance control, with their CAD number.

When clear or stood down, goes to sets the standby point the unit goes back to.

NHS ambulances, the call log and closing

When an NHS ambulance is asked for, tap NHS ambulance requested, then fill in their CAD number and category, and tap Now when the crew arrive and when the patient is handed over. The report shows how long each one took.

Every call has its own log: type an update and press Enter, or tap a quick phrase.

A call closes once every unit on it is clear or stood down and each patient has an outcome (or the call has one, when there was no patient). A closed call can be reopened.

5. Units: status, breaks and off duty

The unit board shows each unit’s status, where it is and since when. For a unit not on a call:

  • Break: a length or a finish time. The board counts down, and beeps and turns amber when the break is over. Back from break ends it.
  • Unavailable: with a reason, such as a vehicle defect or restocking. Available ends it.
  • Off duty: a unit that has not started yet or has finished. On duty brings it back.

You can still assign a unit that is on a break, unavailable or off duty: the CAD asks first, and that ends the break or the time off.

Crew, on any unit, records staff going off and coming on mid-event: the unit’s staff change, and the report lists every crew change.

Radio check, under the board, lists every unit on duty: mark each OK or No reply as you call it, then Log the radio check. The control log gets one entry, such as “Radio check: 15 of 17 OK. No reply: Bravo One.”, with any unit you didn’t reach listed as not checked.

Hospitals

When a hospital goes on divert or is under pressure, set it in the Hospitals card on the CAD tab, with a note. Crews sent there show a warning, a hospital on divert shows in the alerts, and each change goes in the control log.

6. Treatment centres, the control log and major incidents

Every hour, on the Treatment centre tab, record how many patients are in each centre. The CAD reminds you when a centre’s count is due, and picks the next centre due for you.

The Control log tab is for everything that is not a call: “Gates open”, “Spoke to police silver”. Controller changes, hospital statuses, crew changes, missing persons, METHANE messages and casualty counts are logged there by themselves.

Attendance and weather

Each hour, record how many people are on site (from the gates), the temperature and the weather. The report shows the patients seen in each hour and the rate per 1,000 people there, next to the weather.

Lost and vulnerable persons

Lost children and vulnerable adults are kept apart from the medical calls. While someone is missing they stay in the alerts. Record where they were found and who they were handed to; each step is logged and in the report.

A major incident

METHANE (on the CAD tab, or the Control log tab) opens the form: Major incident declared or standby, Exact location, Type, Hazards, Access, Number of casualties and Emergency services. Saved messages go in the control log and the report, and each prints as its own PDF.

As casualty numbers come in, record them by triage category in Major incident casualty count. Each count is timestamped and logged, and the latest fills in the “N” of the next METHANE message.

7. After the event

  1. Close the calls still open.
  2. Finalise the event on the Report tab. The CAD is locked, so nothing can change, and the report is marked final. Unlock asks first and is logged.
  3. Download the report, the call logs (a zip with each call as its own PDF) and, if you want the figures in Excel, the spreadsheet of calls.
  4. Export CAD to keep the whole event as one file. Share report sends the report PDF by email, Teams or WhatsApp where the laptop can.
  5. To see how this event compares with last year’s, choose last year’s CAD file under Compare with another event: the report puts the main figures side by side.
  6. For the next event, Start a new event: the calls and logs are cleared, the setup is kept.

The report

The calls, patients and conveyances, with rates per 1,000 attendees; the comparison with another event; day by day for events over several days; response times overall and by category, with how many met their target; calls by hour, protocol, category, source and location; attendance and weather each hour; outcomes, presentations, ages and alcohol or drugs; conveyances and pre-alerts; NHS ambulance waits; each unit’s workload, breaks, crew changes and time on and off duty; the treatment centre counts; lost and vulnerable persons; the control log; METHANE messages and casualty counts; and a site map with the calls on it. Duplicate calls are left out of the figures.

8. Keeping the CAD safe

The CAD is saved in this browser on this laptop as you go. To make sure a lost or broken laptop never loses the event:

  • Live backup file (Chrome or Edge): on the Report tab, Choose live backup file once — on the laptop or a USB stick — and the CAD writes a copy into it after every change. The line under the CAD buttons says when it last saved. After the browser restarts, press Reconnect.
  • Export CAD at any time saves a copy you choose. Import CAD on any laptop carries on from it, live backup files included. With no live backup file (on an iPad, or before you choose one), the line under the CAD buttons says when you last exported, and after 30 minutes an alert reminds you, with Export now.
  • On an iPad, add the CAD to the home screen (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and always open it from there: Safari can clear a website’s data after 7 days unused, but not a home-screen app’s. The home-screen CAD keeps its own data, apart from Safari’s.
  • Use one browser tab for the CAD. Do not use a private window: it forgets everything when closed.
  • Keep the paper fallback pack printed in control.

9. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
“This browser could not save the last change”Storage is full or this is a private window. Export the CAD at once, then carry on in a normal Chrome or Edge window and import it there.
The live backup has stoppedPress Reconnect on the Report tab, or choose the file again.
No live backup buttonThe browser cannot write to files (an iPad, for one). Use Chrome or Edge on a laptop, or export the CAD often: it reminds you every 30 minutes.
what3words suggestions do not appearThey need the key in Setup and an internet connection. Without them the address is still recorded, with a link to the map.
No beep when a break endsBrowsers only play sound once the page has been clicked. The amber alert still shows.
Everything is greyed outThe event is finalised. Unlock it on the Report tab.
A unit can’t be removed in SetupIt has been on calls. Rename it instead.
